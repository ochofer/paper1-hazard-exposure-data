  WHY IT MOVES THIS WAY, which is the opposite of what was assumed.
  Residual counterparties are NOT concentrated in the restatement bucket.
  They are most concentrated in BUILD-OUT, at 23.6% against 14.2% for
  restatement and 14.7% for methodology. Build-out sits in the denominator
  only. So removing residuals from both sides strips proportionally more
  from the denominator than from the numerator, and R RISES.

  The working assumption throughout was that excluding residuals would
  deflate R and put the pre-committed 25% bar at risk. It does the
  reverse. The restricted measure is the more conservative construction
  and it makes the go decision safer, not thinner.

  A finding in its own right: nearly a quarter of the edges counted as
  the database growing are new links to a residual bucket, which is not
  new ownership information either. Growth in residual buckets is not
  growth in coverage.
