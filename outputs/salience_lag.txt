  A PHRASING CORRECTION FOR THE NOTE. The slow group has been described as
  'two to four and a half years'. The shortest of the four is 670 days,
  which is 1.8 years, not two. The accurate phrase is
  '1.8 to 4.6 years', or the days themselves.

  The two fast cases are the recent, heavily covered transactions: a
  statutory disclosure by a listed Japanese issuer, and a 1.9bn USD
  acquisition by a listed US utility. The four slow ones are older or reach
  the affected entity only through a perimeter. Salience note per case is in
  the evidence file.

  WHAT THIS IS NOT. Six cases cannot establish a correlation. The claim the
  note makes is that the lag is not a constant, which one fast case and one
  slow case would establish, and that its variation lines up with salience,
  which is offered as the reading of six hand-checked cases and labelled as
  such. An offset cannot fix a lag that is not constant, and that is the
  consequence that matters.
