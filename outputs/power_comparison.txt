SENSITIVITY. The assumed standard deviations are the soft inputs,
so both designs are re-scored across a plausible range.

  Design A, 10 years (120 months), against 2.0%/year:
    sd=1.0%/month -> MDE  3.29%/year, power ratio  1.6
    sd=1.5%/month -> MDE  4.93%/year, power ratio  2.5
    sd=2.0%/month -> MDE  6.57%/year, power ratio  3.3
    sd=2.5%/month -> MDE  8.22%/year, power ratio  4.1

  Design B, 75 events, against 1.0% CAR:
    sd=1.5%/event -> MDE  0.52%, power ratio  0.5
    sd=2.0%/event -> MDE  0.69%, power ratio  0.7
    sd=2.5%/event -> MDE  0.87%, power ratio  0.9
    sd=3.0%/event -> MDE  1.04%, power ratio  1.0

CONCLUSION
  Design A at 10 years: power ratio 3.3. Underpowered by roughly 3x.
  Design B at 75 events: power ratio 0.7.
  The event study can detect a plausible effect on this data. The
  cross-sectional premium cannot, and would need decades to.

  Both use the same 328 firms and the same exposure measure, so this
  is a change of question rather than a change of dataset.

  VERDICT: expanding to developed markets does NOT rescue design A.
  Even on the optimistic bound the power ratio at 10 years falls from
  3.3 to 2.6, which is still far above 1.0.

  It helps DESIGN B considerably more, and for a different reason.
  Adding Japan, Australia, Hong Kong and Singapore adds typhoon and
  cyclone exposure, so it raises the EVENT COUNT rather than only the
  firm count, and event count is what drives design B's power.
  Japan alone adds 103 firms and 1,488 operating assets.

  COST, measured. The identifier crosswalk degrades badly:
    ISINs in the ANNA file by country prefix
      Canada 0, Hong Kong 0, Singapore 0, New Zealand 0
      Japan 3,640, Australia 7,104
      against United States 2,281,221 and Germany 4,232,134
    So 73 of the 213 new firms have no ANNA route at all, and Japan's
    103 firms rest on a very thin file. The three-lane crosswalk would
    need a fourth lane before expansion is usable.

WHAT DESIGN B STILL NEEDS, none of it free of difficulty
  1. An event list. NOAA storm data is free. EM-DAT is free for
     academic use. Both need cleaning to asset-relevant events.
  2. Events must be UNANTICIPATED. Hurricanes are forecast days
     ahead, so the event date should be the forecast or track
     announcement, not landfall. Getting this wrong destroys the
     design more quietly than getting the returns wrong.
  3. Asset coordinates, to know which firm was in the path. This is
     the SAME blocker as design A: the GEM sector trackers.
  4. Independence. Two hurricanes in one season hitting the same
     coast are not independent events. Cluster or drop.
