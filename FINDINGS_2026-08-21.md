### Crosswalk faults found, and why they matter

Each of these would have been invisible downstream, since none produces a missing value.

- **Every listing line per firm was being downloaded.** Allianz appeared three times as
  `ALV`, `ALIZY` and `ALIZF`. A portfolio holding all three holds one bet with three
  weights, and near perfectly correlated duplicates inflate cross-sectional precision.
- **Preferred shares entered through name matching.** `UEPEP` stood in for Union Electric
  and its 42 assets; preferred is a bond-like claim and does not carry the equity return.
- **Berkshire resolved to `BRK-A`**, which trades a few hundred shares a day at roughly
  1,500 times the B-share price, giving stale prices and coarse discreteness.
- **Five European firms sat on thin US receipts.** Stora Enso was on a line trading
  $302k a day against $19.4m in Helsinki; also Fortum, Outokumpu, Neste and Henkel. A
  receipt carries an exchange rate move and a stale US close against a European session,
  so it is the wrong series rather than a noisy one.
- **The `lei` column held the literal string `"not found"` for 13 firms**, Chevron among
  them. As a string it is a valid join key, so all 13 were merged into one another.

All corrections are in `config/ticker_overrides.csv` with written reasons.

## 3. Blocking test 1: survivorship

**Result: FMP cannot answer this question, and the reason is decisive.**

Recorded delistings by year: 7 in 2010, 3 in 2012, 10 in 2015, then 1,843 in 2023 and
2,353 in 2025. A 28-fold rise. Delisting rates do not move like that; coverage does. The
list is effectively empty before 2016 and only dense from 2021, so **any rate computed
across the full window from this source is an artefact, not a lower bound.**

What can be said:

| Measure | Value | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Size and venue matched rate, 2021-2025 | 9.6 percent | Numerator matched on dollar volume, denominator on market cap. Not the same filter |
| Annualised | 1.93 percent | |
| Extrapolated to 16 years | 30.9 percent | An assumption, not a measurement |

**The more useful finding reverses the standard story.** Median final-year return against
the market, split by the firm's own final-year dollar volume:

| Final-year dollar volume | n | Median gap | Share negative |
|---|---|---|---|
| under $100k | 32 | -14.6% | 59% |
| $100k to $1m | 16 | -67.9% | 88% |
| $1m to $10m | 13 | +0.7% | 46% |
| **over $10m** | **10** | **+18.1%** | **30%** |

Small firms delist because they fail. Firms of the size in this universe mostly delist
because they are **acquired**, and acquisitions pay a premium. So excluding delisted firms
may bias returns **downward** here rather than upward. With n of 10 this is a hint, not a
finding, and the honest statement is that **the bias is unmeasured at the sizes that
matter**.

Right instrument: CRSP delisting codes via WRDS, complete from 1926 with delisting returns
attached. Access applied for, not yet granted. Free fallbacks: SEC Form 25 filings on
EDGAR for the US names, and the GLEIF `entity_status` field, already on disk.

## 4. Blocking test 2: factor replication

**Passed.** Market beta 0.917 equal-weighted and 1.03 value-weighted, R-squared 0.85,
4,023 days, median 134 firms a day.

Individual betas against published expectations:

| Firm | Beta | Expectation |
|---|---|---|
| Duke Energy | 0.43 | utility, well below 1 |
| Southern Co | 0.44 | utility, well below 1 |
| American Electric Power | 0.45 | utility, well below 1 |
| NextEra | 0.60 | utility, below 1 |
| Exxon Mobil | 0.81 | energy, near 1 |
| Chevron | 0.94 | energy, near 1 |

Ordering is correct and magnitudes match published estimates. **The crosswalk is sound and
the returns are real returns.**

### The alpha, and what it is not

The portfolio carries an alpha of 3.5 to 4.2 percent a year, significant. Two explanations
were eliminated by measurement.

**Not microstructure.** Across equal versus value weighting and daily versus monthly
buy-and-hold, the alpha moved 0.19 percentage points: 3.67, 2.83, 3.93, 3.49. The
Blume-Stambaugh daily-rebalancing bias is not the cause, despite 26 listings trading under
a million dollars a day.

**Not missing factors.** Adding RMW, CMA and momentum *raised* it from 3.49 to 4.18 at t
of 2.52. The CMA loading is -0.225, so the richer model expects even less from these
capital-intensive firms.

### What it is: a period effect

| Subperiod | Alpha, percent a year | t |
|---|---|---|
| 2010-2019, pre-COVID | +1.87 | 0.99 |
| 2020-2022, COVID and energy shock | +10.60 | 2.80 |
| 2023-2025, data-centre demand | +8.31 | 1.86 |

**Over a normal decade this portfolio has no alpha.** The pipeline is not manufacturing a
premium. Everything sits after 2020 and is dateable: the COVID collapse and recovery, the
2022 European gas crisis which handed windfalls to power and energy names, and data-centre
electricity demand repricing the independent power producers.

Subperiods carry 36 observations against 6 factors, so read the alphas as indicative
magnitudes and the t-statistics as weak evidence.

### Why this is a design finding, not a plumbing one

Two thirds of the return variation sits in the last third of the window, so **any
cross-sectional sort will be dominated by it**. Both episodes correlate with the sort
variable by construction: the 2022 crisis was a shock to European energy assets, and the
data-centre trade is concentrated in US independent power producers, of which Vistra,
Constellation, Talen and NRG are all large owners in this universe.

A hazard sort can load on either and report it as a climate risk premium, with a
convincing t-statistic.

## 5. Pre-commitments, made before any hazard variable exists

Recorded here so they cannot be motivated by a result.

1. **Report the full sample as headline**, with pre-2020 and post-2020 splits alongside as
   standard rather than as robustness, and state that the sample contains one large
   energy-market regime change.
2. **Check the overlap** between high-hazard firms and the independent power producers
   before running the sort, and report the result with and without them.
3. **Survivorship is unmeasured at the relevant firm size.** Say so. Do not quote the 9.6
   percent figure as if it described this universe.
4. **Do not shorten the sample window** to fit data availability. A sample chosen by what a
   vendor will sell is a vendor-selected sample and belongs in the limitations section.
5. **A rigorous null result is success.** Report the minimum detectable effect so a null is
   informative.

## 6. What is still missing

**The exposure variable does not exist.** The GEM Ownership Tracker carries no
coordinates: three of nine sheets have a `GEM location ID`, which is a key rather than a
place, and the mine, pipeline, steel and cement sheets have no location field at all.
Physical hazard exposure needs the individual sector trackers, a hazard dataset, the join
and the firm-level aggregation. None of that needs the subscription.

**The ownership snapshot is undated**, with sector vintages spanning roughly sixteen
months. That is look-ahead in the independent variable, which is worse than survivorship
bias in the dependent one because no downstream care repairs it. Waiting on prior releases
from GEM.

**Currency conversion has not been done.** Nine currencies, and GBp is pence.

**Two subsidiary roll-up decisions are open**, Union Electric and Entergy Louisiana,
carrying 69 assets.
